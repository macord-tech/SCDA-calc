Notes
  • Start at the runway and work outward. List each fix with the altitude to be at or above and the distance from the previous one. The first fix is measured from the runway threshold, where the path is 50 ft above the threshold elevation (AC 700-028).
  • The angle is constant from 50 ft over the threshold outward. Each fix is a segment: "Req:" is its altitude minus the altitude of the fix before it (50 ft over the threshold for the first fix), divided by the distance between them. The steepest segment sets the angle for the whole approach, from the threshold out to the top of descent. Because that gradient is at least as steep as every other segment, the path is at or above every fix altitude.
  • Cross Over Hgt is the altitude on the line at each fix, and it is also shown in red on the diagram. The figure shown includes a 100 ft buffer, so the pilot aims to cross each fix no lower than 100 ft above its altitude. The buffer is not part of the calculation: the angle, gradient, rates, MDA crossing and top of descent use the plain fix altitudes. The top of descent altitude is optional. If you enter it, the tool shows how far out it is, and a fix farther out than that shows the top of descent altitude.
  • "MDA crossing" is how far from the runway threshold the path is at the MDA altitude you enter. Descent ratio is the gradient divided by 60. Multiply it by the ground speed in knots to get the rate of descent in ft/min (the gradient times the ground speed in NM per minute, AC 700-028 §5.3). "Back from nearest fix" is how far before the nearest fix inbound of the top of descent the descent starts.

Planning aid built from Transport Canada AC 700-028 Issue 01, Vertical Path Control on Non-Precision Approaches. For operations, use the approach chart and the published descent angle.
